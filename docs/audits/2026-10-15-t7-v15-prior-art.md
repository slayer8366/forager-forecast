## 2. Do other apps focus on burn morels? (the premise of -914)

**Only partly.** Of the 8 products:
- **Four make burns central to their morel offer:** P2, a dedicated burn-morel app; P1, whose morel page leads with burns; and P3 and P4, whose morel guides centre on a wildfire layer.
- **Four map something else:** P5 maps host-tree stands, P6 and P7 map predicted habitat or species, and P8 maps user sightings.

Even the burn-focused products are not burn-only. P1 sells a host-tree density layer and an experimental single-species matsutake habitat layer. P2 has a terrain-based "morel probability" layer.

A fair version of the premise: **the morel-focused US products centre on burn maps; several products also map host trees or habitat, but none found maps mycorrhizal habitat as a group.**

## 3. Is ectomycorrhizal habitat already mapped at stand scale in North America?

**Nothing found maps habitat for mycorrhizal fungi as a group at stand scale (tens to hundreds of metres).** The search was not exhaustive.

The nearest work:
- **P1** maps live-tree density for 22 host species, with an experimental single-species matsutake layer. Its resolution and sources are not stated.
- **P5** maps stands by host tree, linked to single species, for parts of Oregon, Washington and BC.
- **Research** (section 4): plot-level national maps (Jo et al. 2019); pre-settlement potential associations (Swaty et al. 2016); averaged species occupancy (Carroll et al.); and global maps at 10 arcmin and 1 km. All are too coarse for stand scale, or are not habitat.
- **A student suitability map for southern BC** (READ): chanterelle, morel and late fall oyster at 5 km cells, with weights set by the authors' judgement. https://blogs.ubc.ca/bcmushroomforagingguide/?p=81

**Could not determine:** whether any agency in Oregon, Washington or BC has published a stand-level ectomycorrhizal layer.

## 4. Research maps

- **Steidinger, B.S. et al. (2019).** "Climatic controls of decomposition drive the global biogeography of forest-tree symbioses." *Nature* 569: 404–408. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1128-0
  - READ from the Europe PMC record: more than 1.1 million plots; about 60% of tree stems are ectomycorrhizal; subscription access, with no licence shown.
  - An earlier manuscript at UCL Discovery aggregates "to a 1 by 1 degree grid". A Purdue page lists 1-degree AM, EM and NFIXER layers as supplementary zips, with no licence stated.
- **Van Nuland, M.E. et al. (2025).** "Global hotspots of mycorrhizal fungal richness are poorly protected." *Nature* 645: 414–422. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09277-4
  - READ from the full text: about 25,000 soil samples; richness maps for arbuscular and ectomycorrhizal fungi at 1 km²; the article is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.
  - Full maps "available after submitting a data request". The Zenodo record https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14871588 lists CC BY 4.0 for data and MIT for code. Whether it holds the full-resolution maps, and what licence applies to requested maps, could not be determined.
- **Jo, I., Fei, S., Oswalt, C.M., Domke, G.M., Phillips, R.P. (2019).** "Shifts in dominant tree mycorrhizal associations in response to anthropogenic impacts." *Science Advances* 5: eaav6358. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aav6358
  - READ, CC BY-NC 4.0: arbuscular-tree share of basal area at FIA plot level across the contiguous US, with hexagons of about 1,452 km² for change.
  - Data: https://doi.org/10.4231/R76D5R7S (licence not read).
- **Swaty, R., Michael, H.M., Deckert, R., Gehring, C.A. (2016).** "Mapping the potential mycorrhizal associations of the conterminous United States of America." *Fungal Ecology* 24: 139–147. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.funeco.2016.05.005
  - Abstract READ: pre-settlement associations from historical dominant vegetation.
  - Resolution and data not found.
- **Soudzilovskaia, N.A. et al. (2019).** "Global mycorrhizal plant distribution linked to terrestrial carbon stocks." *Nature Communications* 10: 5077. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13019-2
  - READ: biomass fractions by mycorrhizal type at 10 arcmin. The article is CC BY 4.0.
  - Maps: https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.47d7wm387 (no separate data licence stated).
- **Steidinger, B.S. et al. (2020).** *Journal of Biogeography* 47: 772–782. https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.13802
  - From a search summary only: 68 sites, with richness losses forecast up to 26%.
  - The Dryad dataset (https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.h9w0vt4dr, READ) holds community tables, with no maps and no named licence.
- **Carroll, C. et al.** "Pacific Northwest Predicted Survey and Manage Species Occupancy for Current Climate." https://databasin.org/datasets/d81a3c15176b4ee1979ae82665e48eda
  - READ: averaged occupancy for 130 species, 75 of them fungi. Content dated 2006. CC BY 3.0. Resolution not stated. Not ectomycorrhizal-specific.

**Not verified:** any species distribution model for a PNW ectomycorrhizal species.
