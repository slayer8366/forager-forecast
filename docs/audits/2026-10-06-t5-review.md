## Findings

**F1. The transect null does not match B's sample size (result-level; for the owner).**
- `seam.py:78-87` builds each side's null from every transect with any finite within-country step
  (`rows`).
- `seam.py:100` takes B over the transects with a finite border step only.
- **Total cover:** both sets are 25, so it is unaffected.
- **The three shares:** B is over 7 transects, while the nulls are over 23 (US) and 24 (Canada).
  Observed: `2026-10-06-t5-review/rerun_transects.out.txt`, the `null_rows_*` fields.
- A median of 7 draws is wider than a median of 24, so a threshold read from the 24-draw null is
  too low for B.
- **Simulation** (`null_mismatch_sim.py.txt` and its `.out.txt`; 400 trials per case,
  independent normal steps, no real step):

  | Case | Fired |
  | --- | --- |
  | 25 border steps against 25 null rows | 0.0425 |
  | 7 against 24 | **0.165** |
  | 7 against the same 7 | 0.055 |

- **On the real layers**, restricted to the same 7 transects with the same seed and draw count,
  the threshold becomes 0.1199:
  - Douglas-fir share: B 0.0831 (D88 build) and 0.0875 (first build). Both are under that
    threshold, so the verdict is **no step**, not an artifact.
  - Conifer and broadleaf: no step under either null.
  - The figures are in `rerun_transects.out.txt`, under `matched_n_*`.
- **Why the builder's test did not catch it.** The test
  `test_with_no_step_the_verdict_fires_about_one_time_in_twenty_or_less` (`tests/test_seam.py:83-95`)
  draws 25 border steps against 25 rows. It never sees the unequal case, which is the only case
  that could fail it. It is the "never saw the data that could fail it" family in Forager's
  CLAUDE.md.
- **Caveat.** The simulation assumes independent, identically distributed steps. Real
  within-country steps are spatially correlated. That is inferred, and its effect on the rate was
  not measured.
- **Proposed:**
  - The owner chooses between two courses:
    - keep the rule as filed (verify report section 7), with the Douglas-fir artifact recorded
      as weak;
    - or rule a matched-n null, which re-runs the verdicts.
  - Either way, a test with unequal counts should be added once the rule is settled.
  - I did not add that test: it would either fail against the filed rule or pin a rule nobody has
    ruled on.

**F2. Big trees get no crown, or a shrinking one, and are dropped silently.**
- `crown_cover.py:209-210` skips a tree whose Equation 3 width is zero or negative, and counts
  nothing.
- In the strip's plots, 29 live tree rows are dropped this way (`zero_width_trees.out.txt`):
  - 28 western redcedar, at d.b.h. of about 74 in and up. The quadratic peaks near 34 in and
    crosses zero near 74.
  - 1 Douglas-fir, at about 107 in and up.
- 1,500 tree rows of the commonly used species lie beyond the largest d.b.h. in Bechtold's
  Table 1 for their coefficients (`independent_cells.out.txt`, `facts`). Equation 3 is applied
  there by extrapolation, and neither report mentions it.
- **Effect.** Under D88 this touches only the genus split, not the total. The size of the effect
  was not measured.
- **Proposed.** This is a change to D87's formula (for example, capping D at Table 1's maximum),
  so it is the owner's call. Separately, the drop should be counted, as surrogates are, which
  matches Forager's rule against silent fallbacks.

**F3. The SCANFI total is the sum of the ten classes, not SCANFI's own total closure layer.**
- SCANFI v2 publishes `SCANFI_att_closure_XXXX_v2_20260119.tif`, "Total crown closure (%)". This is
  from the readme, read 13:17:55 UTC (line 24).
- T5 uses the sum of the ten species layers instead (`t5_layer.py:456`).
- The readme's examples imply that the classes sum to the total. Whether they do in this strip was
  **not checked**, because it needs a fetch.
- This is the Canadian counterpart of D88's question.
- **Proposed:** an owner item. A one-window comparison on the next fetch would answer it.

**F4. The D88 denominator was untested, and is closed here.**
- `t5_layer.py:512` divides the US genus cover by the regridded split total, not by the cover
  total. This is what keeps a no-split plot "left out of the shares, never counted as zero"
  (completion report, line 205).
- My revert to `us_cover["total"]` did not bite. No test touched a cell mixing split and no-split
  pixels.
- The protocol says the reviewer "may close small gaps in its own commit and list them in the
  review: a missing index row, a typo, a missing test, a missing header field". Under that rule I
  added
  `test_a_cell_mixing_split_and_unsplit_canopy_takes_its_share_from_the_split_part`
  (`tests/test_t5_layer.py:426-439`). It asserts that the sample holds mixed cells before it
  asserts their share.
- It passes forward, and the revert bites with it (`revert_review_2_results.json`).

  uses (36, listed in `independent_cells.out.txt`).
- Crookston and Stage 1999: eq. 1, C′ = 100(Σ pᵢaᵢ)A⁻¹ with A = 43560; eq. 2,
  C = 100[1 − exp(−.01 C′)]; "the formula for a circle as a function of crown radius". All as
  coded (`crown_cover.py:211-217`).
