## Prediction (mechanism), written before any computation

What had been read when this was written: the fit report
(`docs/audits/2026-10-16-t7-fit-report.md`), the fix commit `41f9c79` (diff and message),
`src/forager_forecast/t7/{model,metrics,aoa,folds,presences,background,keying}.py`,
`scripts/t7_fit.py`, the proposals' P3 and P6 to P10, DECISIONS D133 to D144, the two dispatches,
RECORD -1126, -1127, -1134 and -1137, the smoke pass's own figures
(`/mnt/work/Zynergy/forecast-data/t7/smoke-evidence/fit_report.json`, which is the fit's work
folder, within the read scope) and the committed tuning grid. Nothing had been computed.

1. **Why the habitat gain is small by construction.** Effort enters as a covariate that is constant
   over every 250 m cell of a 0.1° weather cell, is forced additive (interaction constraint) and
   rising (`model.py:40-46`). Presences sit in the frame's well-searched cells; the 500,000
   background cells are spread evenly over the frame's eligible cells. The pooled AUC counts
   presence-against-background pairs, and most of those pairs are in *different* weather cells, where
   effort alone already separates them. The pairs that habitat can win, those inside one weather
   cell, are a small share of the total, so a real 250 m habitat signal can only move the pooled AUC
   by a little. Prediction: model A's AUC restricted to pairs inside one weather cell is exactly 0.5
   (it is constant within a cell), and the full model's within-cell AUC is well above 0.5, somewhere
   near 0.60 to 0.70. That within-cell figure, not the 0.004, is the measure of habitat beyond
   effort, and the map at held effort is made of exactly that within-cell part.
2. **The map against the effort surface.** The map is the model at one effort value, so effort's
   additive part is gone from it by construction; what remains can still mirror where people search
   if the forest types near the searched lowlands are what the model learned to score high. The
   effort-only map at held effort is flat, so the comparison the question asks for is the full map
   against the effort surface itself (model A's score at each cell's own effort ranks the same as
   log effort, because A is monotone in its single input). Prediction: a positive, moderate Spearman
   correlation over shaded cells, between about 0.25 and 0.55. The largest disagreements: west-side
   forest far from the Puget-Willamette corridor (Olympics, Coast Range, western Cascades interior)
   high on the map and low in effort; forest around east-side towns (Spokane, Boise, Bend, Missoula)
   high in effort and lower on the map. Cells with no canopy are masked (code 4), so the map cannot
   be "forest versus non-forest"; it will be "which forest": a west-side, Douglas-fir and hemlock,
   moderately acid soil gradient scoring highest, dry pine and Rockies forest lowest.
3. **Mycorrhizal-specific or not.** The comparison group (*Laetiporus*, wood rot on hardwoods) gains
   more over A for two reasons I expect to see in the numbers: its A baseline is lower (606
   presences, AUC 0.844), and its gain over B comes from the hardwood bands (*Quercus*, *Arbutus*,
   *Chrysolepis*, broadleaf share) and pH, which name its substrate as surely as the conifer bands
   name a mycorrhizal host. The 0.23 rank correlation says the two maps differ in *which* forest they
   score high, which is what forest-type inputs give any forest fungus. The design cannot separate
   "host tree" from "forest type", so the evidence supports "reports of this group by forest type",
   not "mycorrhizal habitat". The rank correlation of 0.23 should reproduce from the two boosters'
   scores; the comparison's A, B and full AUCs should reproduce from the cache.
4. **The weighting fix.** Before the fix, the comparison group's weights summed to 2 over 500,606
   points: a presence's hessian is about 0.25 × 1/606 ≈ 4 × 10⁻⁴ and a background point's about
   0.25 × 1/500,000 ≈ 5 × 10⁻⁷, so a leaf of 1,000 background points carries about 5 × 10⁻⁴ of
   hessian, under LightGBM's default `min_sum_hessian_in_leaf` of 10⁻³. Splits are refused; B
   reduces to A, as the smoke pass saw. For the main group the total is about 90 over 515,624
   points; a 1,000-point leaf carries about 0.04 of hessian, above the floor, so it splits. With
   `lambda_l2 = 10` the leaf values are shrunk by a factor of order (0.04 + 10) / 0.04, but shrinking
   every leaf by nearly the same factor barely changes the *ranking*, which is what the AUC reads.
   So: the fix is mechanically required for the comparison group; the main group's unfixed fit did
   split and ranked nearly as the fixed one does (the smoke pass's own file says A 0.8769, B 0.8787,
   full 0.8803 at 50,000 background; the full run gives 0.8773, 0.8788, 0.8815). The smoke pass
   failed P10 on one of four conditions, the all-blocks full-minus-B interval
   (−0.00027 to +0.0029, 50 resamples). Prediction for the cheap re-run (unfixed weights, the full
   run's chosen configurations, no re-tuning): pooled AUCs within about 0.002 of the fixed ones,
   full-minus-A wholly above zero, full-minus-B's lower bound near zero either way. The fix changes
   no M3 share, no P3 share, no background draw, no P10 bar and no AOA rule; it changes the AOA
   threshold's *value* only through the final model's gains. The fix was triggered by a verdict
   but has no free parameter to tune, and the test it came with asserts scale invariance, not a
   verdict; I expect to find it stands on its own.
5. **Revert check.** With `unit_mean_weights` taken out of `train`, the new test fails at its first
