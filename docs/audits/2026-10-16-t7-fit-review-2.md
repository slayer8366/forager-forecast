### What a reader of this map is told that the effort surface would not (INFERRED from the figures above)

The effort surface says where fungi get reported: the I-5 corridor, the Puget and Willamette
lowlands, the coast. The map says something nearly independent of that: holding search equal,
the forest types most associated with reports of these genera are the interior conifer forests
of the Rockies and the Cascade crest, and the lowland forests where most reports come from score
middling. That is a statement the effort surface does not make, and it is not the effort surface
redrawn. Two cautions go with it:

1. It is a between-cell inference as much as a within-cell one. The additive, rising effort term
   and the habitat term are not separately identified between weather cells: where effort is
   high the model can credit the reports to effort, where effort is low and reports still occur
   it credits them to habitat. The block ranking above (searched lowlands low, unsearched
   mountains high) is the pattern that confounding would produce, and the within-cell AUC of
   0.548 says the pairs inside cells constrain the habitat term only weakly. I cannot separate
   "the Rockies are better habitat for these genera" from "the Rockies are where reports outrun
   the effort model's expectation" with this design, and nor could the coder.
2. A forager reading the map would see the Olympic and Coast Range forest shaded no darker than
   Idaho's batholith (the first being, from general knowledge and no source read here, the
   region's best-known chanterelle and bolete country). Whether that is a finding or an artefact
   of the effort model's shape is the first question the map raises, and these data do not
   answer it.

### What the evidence supports, and my recommendation on wording

For "mycorrhizal habitat" to be the right label, the habitat inputs would have to help the
mycorrhizal group in a way they do not help a fungus with no host relationship. The design has
one such test, the comparison group, and it points the other way: the wood rotter gains six
times as much pooled AUC from the same inputs (+0.023 against +0.004), has the same within-cell
lift (0.542 against 0.548), and ends with a different map (0.23). A different map is expected
whatever the mechanism, because the two groups sit in different forests; it is not evidence of
host specificity. The inputs describe forest type (which genera, how much canopy, what soil pH),
and a forest-type model separates reports of any forest fungus from background. Nothing in the
run shows the mycorrhizal map to be about hosts rather than forest type, and the one comparison
available says the inputs are at least as useful without a host relationship.

Recommendation: do not label it "mycorrhizal habitat". The most the evidence supports is a
sentence of the form "where the forest's tree mix and soil most resemble the forest around
reports of these mushroom genera, with search effort held equal", and a label along the lines of
"forest types associated with reports of mycorrhizal mushroom genera" or the dispatch's
"forest-associated fungal reporting". "Habitat" alone over-reads a 0.004 pooled and 0.55
within-cell signal; "mycorrhizal" names the group whose records were used, which is fair, but as
an adjective on "habitat" it claims the host mechanism the data do not show. The owner chooses
the words; this is the evidence.

## Question 3. The weighting fix after a failed trial

### Mechanism (COMPUTED from the rebuilt rows; INFERRED where marked)

Before `41f9c79` the comparison group's weights were 1/606 per presence and 1/500,000 per
background point (one M3 unit each side; `presences.py` `genus_weights`, `background.py`
`background_weights`). A binary-objective hessian is w·p(1−p) ≤ w/4, so a leaf of
`min_data_in_leaf = 1000` background points carries at most 1000 × 0.25 / 500,000 = 5 × 10⁻⁴ of
hessian, under LightGBM's default `min_sum_hessian_in_leaf` of 10⁻³ (INFERRED from LightGBM's
documented default; `lgb_params` does not set it, `model.py:28-46`). Splits on background-heavy
leaves are refused, which is why the smoke pass logged identical A and B predictions for the
comparison group (`fit_log.txt:121`, both 0.79483). For the main run the total weight was 90 over
515,624 rows, so a 1,000-point leaf carries about 0.04 of hessian, above the floor, and the
smoke pass's own figures show its unfixed main fit splitting on habitat at c00 (READ,
`smoke-evidence/fit_report.json`: A 0.8769, B 0.8787, base 0.8798, full 0.8803, at a tenth of the
background, two configurations, 50 resamples). But the hessian floor is not the only scale-bound
term: with `lambda_l2 = 10` beside a per-leaf hessian of 0.04, a split's gain is about G²/λ, so
the re-run below finds that at c16 the unfixed main fit splits on nothing but effort.

### Does the fix change any ruled share, threshold or sample?

- M3 unit shares, P3's presence-to-background balance, the background draw, the P10 bar and the
  AOA rule: unchanged. `unit_mean_weights` multiplies every weight by one scalar
  (`model.py:50-59`); a weighted AUC, the weighted log loss used for tuning and the bootstrap are
  invariant to it (my `wauc` on `w` and on `w × len(w)/w.sum()` agree exactly).
- What it does change: the fitted trees (the hessian floor and the L2 penalty act on the weights'
  scale), hence every prediction and every gain, and so the AOA threshold's *value* through the
  gains (0.2447 in the smoke pass, 0.1982 in the full run; those two also differ in background
  size and configuration count, so that is not the fix's effect alone).

### Does the main group's result depend on it? (`unfixed_rerun.py`)

Control first: model A re-run by my script with the fixed weights at the manifest's chosen
configurations reproduces the cache to the last bit (`max_abs_diff_vs_cache_A = 0.0`,
`unfixed_rerun_fixed_A.json`), so the parameter construction and LightGBM's determinism are
confirmed and any difference below is the weights' scale and nothing else.

Then the same three models with the **unfixed** weights (sum 90, mean 1.75 × 10⁻⁴), at the chosen
configurations (c15 or c16 for A, c16 for B and full), no re-tuning, 24 folds each
(`unfixed_rerun_unfixed_A_B_full.json`):

| unfixed weights, chosen configurations | A | B | full |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| pooled held-out AUC | 0.8762 | 0.8681 | 0.8681 |
| distinct held-out predictions | 298 | 273 | 273 |

full − A: −0.0081 [−0.0113, −0.0050]; full − B: exactly 0 (identical predictions); forested the
same. **At c16 with the unfixed weights, B and the full model are the same model: no split on any
habitat feature, only on `log_effort`.** So the main group's unfixed fit at its chosen
configuration does not split on habitat at all, and P10 fails trivially. My prediction 4 was
wrong for this configuration: I expected the main group's unfixed fit to split and rank nearly
as the fixed one, from the smoke pass's figures. The smoke pass ran at c00 (15 leaves, 50 per
leaf, rate 0.1, L2 10, 500 rounds), where habitat splits were taken; c16 (1,000 per leaf, rate
0.03, 200 rounds) is where the tuning landed with the fixed weights, and there the unfixed weights
leave no habitat split. INFERRED why: with L2 = 10 against a per-leaf hessian of order 0.04 a
split's gain is about G²/λ, not normalised by the hessian, and effort's larger gradient sum wins
every root split; the interaction constraint then keeps each tree on effort alone. (With the
fixed weights the per-leaf hessian is of order 250, L2 is small beside it, and gains are
hessian-normalised.)

What this answers, and what it does not:
- The fix is mechanically required for the main group as well as for the comparison group, at
  the configuration the fixed run chose. The report's "was affected less, but was affected"
  understates it.
- Whether an unfixed run *with its own tuning* would have passed P10 is not settled by this cheap
  run: its tuning would have chosen among configurations where habitat does split (the smoke
  pass, c00, gave +0.0034 for full − A and +0.0016 for full − B). Re-tuning unfixed is about 12
  hours of fits and was not done.
- The unfixed fit is not a slightly worse version of the fixed one; it is a model whose outcome
  swings with the configuration because the regularisation is on the wrong scale. That is the
  reason the fix is right, independently of what either run scored.

### Was it tuned to the verdict? (INFERRED)

The trigger was a verdict ("P10: fail"), which is why the question is fair. Against tuning: the
smoke pass failed one condition of four (all-blocks full − B, lower bound −0.00027, 50 resamples,
a tenth of the background) while the other three passed, so the unfixed main run was already at
the bar's edge; the defect is specific and visible with no verdict in view (B identical to A for
the comparison group); the fix has no free parameter (a mean of 1 is the scale under which
LightGBM's defaults mean what they say); and its test asserts scale invariance, not a figure.
The re-run above settles it: with the unfixed weights the chosen configuration cannot split on
habitat at all, so the fix was not a nudge toward a pass but the condition for the habitat
models to exist. I find no evidence that it was tuned to the verdict.

### The two one-line answers

**Does the evidence support publishing this as a pilot habitat layer, and under what wording?**
It supports a pilot layer of "forest types associated with reports of mycorrhizal mushroom
genera, with search effort held equal", shown with the owner's "not a forecast, not yet
reviewed" style of label and with the frame outline so a reader sees that the darkest region is
extension into unsearched forest; it does not support the words "mycorrhizal habitat", and it
does not support any reading of the shading as where mushrooms are more likely.

**What would most strengthen the habitat signal?** A within-cell test built into the bar (pairs
inside one weather cell, or a finer effort layer such as distance to roads and trails so the
between-cell confounding is broken), together with a second comparison group chosen to share the
forests but not the hosts, so that "host" and "forest type" can be told apart; after that, more
presences in the interior blocks where the map is most confident and the data thinnest.
