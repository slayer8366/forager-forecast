**What T6 is for, in one paragraph.** People report mushrooms where and when they go out, so a
cell-week with many reports may just be a cell-week with many people in it. T6 builds an "effort
surface": for every weather cell and week, how much looking we expect happened there, from where
people go, which year it is (the apps keep growing), the time of year, and the day of the week. T7
puts that surface into the habitat model as a covariate and then predicts with effort held at one
level everywhere, so the map shows habitat and not crowds (Warton, Renner and Ramp 2013, PLoS ONE
8:e79168, "conditioning on a common level of bias"). T8 uses it beside the weather triggers.
TASKS.md T6's Verify: the surface reproduces the weekend effect and beats a constant-effort model on
held-out deviance.

## The choices, each with a recommendation

### 1. The unit: which cells, which weeks, which area

**Recommended:** the 0.1° ERA5-Land weather cell (`cells.cell_for`, D46, D63, D64) × ISO week
(`cells.iso_week_of`), split into two day-types inside each week (choice 4), over the whole of D26's
area, the United States and Canada (D47, D72). Weeks: the 573 ISO weeks lying wholly inside 2015-01-01
to 2025-12-31, 2015-W02 to 2025-W52. The two partial weeks, 2015-W01 (four days) and 2026-W01 (three
days), are left out, because a week with missing days would read as low effort. Week 53 occurs in 2015
and 2020 and is kept, folded into week 52's seasonal slot.

Cells: every cell with at least one fungal observer-day in 2015 to 2025 (choice 2) is in the frame,
with an explicit zero for every week it has none. A cell no one ever reported from has no information
about how many people go there and is not in the frame; T7 holds effort constant at prediction, so it
never needs a value there.

Alaska and Hawaii are kept: the effort surface does not use tree layers, and T7 masks Alaska itself (SPEC.md,
D31).

Why: the weather cell and ISO week are the unit sighting chance is defined on (D11, D12, SPEC.md;
`cell_weeks.py`), so the covariate lines up with T7 and T8 with no regridding. Continental, because
the fit in choice 5 works from totals and is cheap at any size (section "Size and memory"), and
because T7 fits across the study area (D100).

**Alternatives:** (a) the two T1 boxes only, as a cheaper first region (T8 would then have no
surface outside them); (b) the 0.25° ERA5 cell (precipitation's grid; coarser than the unit sighting
chance is defined on); (c) include the partial weeks with a days-covered adjustment (more code for
seven days in eleven years).

### 2. The response: what "effort" is counted as

**Recommended: distinct observer-days.** In each cell, week and day-type, the number of different
(observer, day) pairs among all fungal records that pass the step list. One person posting 40 photos
on one walk is one outing, not 40.

Step list (a new one, "T6 effort", run by the existing pipeline): not user-obscured; coordinate
uncertainty present and at most 1,000 m; date kept as given (D97, D98); duplicates by D27's
observer-duplicate key (taxon, observer, cell, day). The 1,000 m limit is T1's headline (D33) and is
small against an 11 km cell; 250 m (R6) governs habitat training records in T7, which is a different
question from "was someone in this cell".

Observer: `recordedBy` as written, trimmed. A record with an empty `recordedBy` counts as one
anonymous observer per dataset, so all such records from one dataset on one day in one cell are one
observer-day. This is a stated rule, not a measured one.

Which duplicate key, and why it does not move the response: dropping a duplicate under the observer
key never removes an observer's last record for a cell and day, so observer-days are the same with or
without that step. The event key (taxon, cell, day) would drop a second person's record of the same
species and lose their observer-day, so it is not used here. Both keys' record counts are reported,
as D27 asks.

Why observer-days: records count outings times species found, so a fruiting flush raises the record
count even with the same number of people out; observer-days are the nearest thing these data have to
"a person went looking" (the same reasoning that makes eBird analyses count checklists rather than
birds: Johnston et al. 2021, Diversity and Distributions 27:1265; from memory, not opened).

**Alternatives:** (a) records per cell-week after the event key (D27's modelling key; mixes outings
with how many species were up); (b) distinct observers per cell-week (loses repeat visits); (c) the
250 m limit (R6) or 5,000 m (D33's sensitivity limit) instead of 1,000 m; (d) `recordedByID` instead
of `recordedBy` (often empty; not measured).

### 3. The benchmark taxon

The problem a benchmark solves: all-fungal reports peak when mushrooms are up. If the effort surface's
time-of-year shape is taken from all fungal reports, it carries the fruiting season inside it, and T8,
which looks for weather that brings mushrooms up, would be handed a covariate that already holds part
of the answer. A benchmark is a group whose reports follow people, not fruiting.

**Recommended: lichens, as class Lecanoromycetes** (GBIF backbone key 180, ACCEPTED, kingdom Fungi;
`api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Lecanoromycetes`, read 2026-10-06 20:06 UTC). Lichens are
lichenized fungi, so they are in D26's download already; they are visible all year and do not flush
after rain the way mushrooms do, which is the property Capinha et al. chose pines for. How it enters:
the time-of-year shape of the effort surface comes from lichen observer-days; where people go, the
year and the weekday come from all fungal observer-days (choice 5).

Known weaknesses, stated now so they are not discovered later: (i) not every Lecanoromycetes species
is a lichen and not every lichen is in Lecanoromycetes; it is a class that is mostly lichens, chosen
because the download carries `classKey` and one key is a rule anyone can check; (ii) lichen observers
are a smaller, more specialist crowd, and some may switch to lichens in winter when mushrooms are gone,
which would make the lichen season run opposite to effort; (iii) the key must be confirmed against the
checklist the download used (`d7dddbf4-…` in the DOI record) before use, by reading `classKey`
beside `class` in the zip. A check is proposed: the lichen season and the all-fungi season are shown
side by side by latitude band, as a figure with no verdict.

**Alternatives:** (a) Pinus, as the cited study did: the cleanest benchmark, but a new GBIF download
of plant records, which needs its own dispatch and the owner's word; (b) a long-lived bracket fungus
that stays visible for months (for example Trametes versicolor, turkey tail; it does respond to
weather, less than mushrooms); (c) no benchmark: the time-of-year shape comes from all fungal
observer-days, with the T8 risk above accepted and written down; this changes TASKS.md T6's "Does",
so it needs the owner.

### 4. The weekday term, and how "the weekend effect" is tested

A weekly unit cannot see weekdays unless the week is split. **Recommended:** each cell-week becomes
two rows, weekend (Saturday and Sunday, 2 days) and weekday (Monday to Friday, 5 days), each with its
own observer-day count and with the number of days as its exposure. The model's weekday term is then
the ratio "observer-days per weekend day ÷ observer-days per weekday day". The effort for the whole
week, which T7 and T8 read, is the sum of the two.

The weekend test, fixed now:

- **Pass condition 1:** the fitted weekend-to-weekday ratio is above 1, and its 95% interval from a
  bootstrap clustered by cell (1,000 resamples, seed 20260918, D31) lies wholly above 1.
- **Pass condition 2:** on held-out years (choice 6), adding the weekday term to the model lowers the
  pooled held-out deviance, and the clustered bootstrap interval of that reduction excludes zero.
- **Shown beside, no verdict:** the ratio by year, by latitude band, and in the lichen benchmark.

Source for expecting it: Courter et al. 2013, "Weekend bias in Citizen Science data reporting:
implications for phenology studies", International Journal of Biometeorology 57:715 (found by search,
abstract-level), and Capinha et al. above, whose pine benchmark recorded more at weekends.

**Alternatives:** (a) seven day-of-week levels (Capinha et al. also found a Wednesday rise; 3.5 times
the rows, no change to the method); (b) public holidays counted as weekend days (needs a US and a
Canadian holiday list per province; not measured); (c) the weekend share of the week's records as a
covariate (a share is undefined in a week with no records, which is most cell-weeks).

### 5. The model family and its terms

**Recommended: a Poisson log-linear model with four multiplicative parts,**

expected observer-days in (cell c, year y, ISO week w, day-type d)
= cell level(c) × year level(y) × season(latitude band of c, week-of-year of w) × day-type level(d) × days(d),

where the latitude band is 5° wide on multiples of 5° (15 to 20 N, 20 to 25 N, … to 80 to 85 N; Hawaii
is in the frame through GADM's USA tag, D72) so the season can differ between Georgia and Alberta. With these four parts only, the best-fitting values
depend on nothing but four sets of totals (by cell, by year, by band and week-of-year, by day-type),
and are found by iterative proportional fitting, the standard method for this kind of model (Bishop,
Fienberg and Holland 1975, *Discrete Multivariate Analysis*, chapter 3; from memory, not opened).
No table of zeros is ever built; that is what keeps a continental fit inside the memory ceiling.

With the benchmark (choice 3): the same model is fitted twice, once to all fungal observer-days and
once to lichen observer-days. The effort surface takes cell, year and day-type from the first and the
season from the second, rescaled so the year's total is unchanged.

Sparse cells: a cell with very few reports gets a cell level pulled toward its latitude band's
average, by adding λ "pseudo-weeks" of the band's average to its totals (the empirical Bayes
smoothing of small-area rates, Clayton and Kaldor 1987, Biometrics 43:671; from memory, not
opened). Without it a cell with one report in eleven years is read as exactly that.

Spread beyond Poisson: real counts vary more than Poisson allows. The fitted values are unaffected;
intervals come from the bootstrap clustered by cell, not from Poisson's own formula (the
quasi-Poisson reasoning in Ver Hoef and Boveng 2007, Ecology 88:2766; from memory, not opened).

Package: **no new runtime package.** The fit is a few dozen lines of numpy (2.5.3, already locked);
numpy and scipy are in `uv.lock` today through lightgbm. As an independent check, the test suite
would compare the fit with statsmodels' Poisson GLM on small tables: **statsmodels 0.15.0, pinned
exactly as a dev dependency by uv** (PyPI lists cp314 wheels for 0.15.0, BSD-3-Clause, read
2026-10-06; it brings pandas and patsy into the dev group). Not installed yet.

**Alternatives:** (a) a negative-binomial GLM or a GAM over every cell-week (statsmodels, pyGAM):
models the extra spread directly, but needs the full table with its zeros, up to about 276 million rows
(section "Size and memory"), far past 6 GB; (b) LightGBM with a Poisson objective (already pinned,
handles interactions, but its weekday effect is not a single readable ratio and it needs the full
table too); (c) one season curve for the whole continent (simpler, misstates the south).

### 6. Held-out deviance, the seed, the comparison and the tuning grid

**Held-out scheme, recommended:** leave one year out over 2015 to 2025, 11 folds, as D33 fixed for
T1. Each fold fits on ten years and scores the eleventh. Scored on the held-out year: every frame
cell × every week of that year × both day-types, zeros included, by Poisson deviance. The headline is
one pooled deviance over all folds, not an average of per-fold values (D33's reasoning), with a
per-fold table beside it.

The held-out year's own total of observer-days is given to every model scored, so the comparison is
about where, when and on which days, not about guessing the app's growth that year. Shown beside, no
verdict: the same scores with the year's level carried from its neighbouring years instead.

**Constant-effort comparison:** every frame cell-day in the held-out year gets the same expected
observer-days (the year's total spread evenly). **Pass condition:** the full surface's pooled held-out
deviance is lower than the constant model's, and the 95% interval of the difference from a bootstrap
clustered by cell (1,000 resamples, seed 20260918) excludes zero. A ladder is reported beside it, each
step scored the same way: constant; + cell; + year; + season; + day-type (the full fungal fit); and the
benchmark-season surface. The ladder shows what each part adds, so a pass that is all "people go to
some places more than others" is visible as such.

Expected in advance, written now: the benchmark-season surface will fit all-fungal counts less well
than the fungal-season one, because the fruiting season is real in those counts. That is the
intended trade (choice 3), and the pass condition is judged on the recommended surface, the
benchmark-season one.

**Tuning grid (D31), fixed now** (also in `2026-10-06-t6-proposal/tuning_grid.json`):

- λ, the sparse-cell pull (pseudo-weeks of the band average): 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10.
- Season smoothing, a centred moving average over week-of-year that wraps around the year end: 1
  (none), 3, 5, 9 weeks.

5 × 4 = 20 configurations, exactly D31's budget, so every configuration is tried and no random draw
is needed to choose them. The seed 20260918 is used for every random draw in T6 (the bootstraps). The
configuration is chosen inside each outer fold by an inner leave-one-year-out over that fold's ten
training years, by pooled Poisson deviance. λ = 0 is not in the grid: it gives a cell seen only in the
held-out year an expected value of zero and an infinite deviance.

**A departure to rule on:** D31 says configurations are chosen by Brier score. Brier is for a yes/no
outcome; T6's outcome is a count, and the T6 Verify itself is stated in deviance. Recommended: choose
by deviance for T6, recorded as a new row that names D31 and leaves it unchanged for the calendar and
full models. Alternative: turn each cell-week into "any report, yes or no" and score Brier (throws
away the counts the surface is built from).

### 7. The output T7 and T8 read

**Recommended:** the surface is stored as its parts, not as a filled table: four small CSV files
(cell level by cell id, year level, season by latitude band and week-of-year, day-type level), one
set for the all-fungi fit and one for the lichen fit, plus `effort_manifest.json` (input DOI and zip
sha256, step-list counts, chosen configuration, seed, code commit, licence track). One function,
`effort(cell, iso_week)` in `src/forager_forecast/effort.py`, returns expected observer-days for that
cell-week (both day-types summed) and its log, the form a covariate takes. A cell outside the frame
returns an explicit "no effort value" and never a made-up one. Files go under
`data/t6/` (git-ignored, like D26's data) with their sha256 filed in the completion report.

The value at which T7 holds effort constant is T7's choice and is not made here.

**Alternatives:** (a) a filled cell × week table (up to about 140 million values; needs pyarrow and
Parquet, a new dependency); (b) a CSV of only the cell-weeks with reports (misses zeros, which are
the point).

## Size and memory (dispatch item 4)

Ceiling: about 6 GB on this 11 GB laptop; at the time of writing `free -g` showed 8 GB available and
the 3 GB swap full, so the real margin is smaller than the total suggests.

- **The frame, if it were built in full:** land cells in the US and Canada at 0.1° are about 240,000
  (about 18.1 million km² of land, from memory, over a cell of 62 to 101 km² between 60 and 35 N,
  computed), × 573 weeks × 2 day-types = up to about 276 million rows. At 16 bytes a row that is about
  4.4 GB before any model matrix, which is why choice 5 avoids it. The frame is cells with reports, so
  the real figure is lower, by an amount not yet counted.
- **What the recommended fit holds:** the totals (at most about 240,000 cell values, 11 years, about
  14 bands × 53 weeks, 2 day-types), and the list of non-zero (cell, week, day-type) counts for
  scoring, at most one entry per observer-day and so at most 2.48 million entries, about 40 MB at 16
  bytes. Zeros enter the deviance through the sum of expected values, which is a product of totals
  and needs no table.
- **The loading pass:** the existing pipeline keeps one `Record` per surviving duplicate key until
  the end. The two independent recounts of D26 filed today peaked at 481 MB and 852 MB doing the same
  kind of pass (D27-D29 review; D28 review). Estimate for T6: under 1.5 GB, not yet measured.
- **Expected peak, estimated:** under 2 GB. 22 fits per configuration per fold are on totals and take
  seconds.
- **How it stays under:** the zip is read in place, streamed, never extracted; no full cell-week
  table is built; the run logs its peak memory (`/usr/bin/time -v`) and a watchdog reading
  `/proc/self/status` stops it with a message if resident memory passes 5 GB; one heavy job at a time,
  and no build or emulator alongside.
