## For the owner: are lichens fit as T8's benchmark?

**Plain judgement: fit for autumn groups, not as built for spring groups such as morels, and with
little effect north of 50 N.** This is a judgement, and nothing has been changed.

What the data show (`refit_results.json`, `beside_lichen_spring`; `flat_season_results.json`;
builder's `season_comparison_by_band`):

- **Between 30 N and 50 N, the lichen spring peak is four days a year.** Every year from 2018 to
  2025, the four busiest lichen days form one Friday-to-Monday block at the end of April or start
  of May: 2018-04-27 to 04-30, 2019-04-26 to 29, 2020-04-24 to 27, 2021-04-30 to 05-03,
  2022-04-29 to 05-02, 2023-04-28 to 05-01 (with 2023-01-01 in the top four), 2024-04-26 to 29,
  2025-04-25 to 28. Those days hold 4.6% of the bands' lichen outings. With them taken out, ISO
  week 17 falls from 5,521 to 1,380 lichen outings, and the year is nearly flat: about 1,700 to
  2,700 a week, a little higher in spring and autumn, lowest in winter. That the blocks are
  iNaturalist's City Nature Challenge is **inferred**: the dates match the challenge dates as I
  remember them, and I did not open a source. All-fungi outings rise that week too (21,040 against
  12,059 and 16,767 either side), so the event is real effort. But lichens rise about twice as
  much in proportion, and the 3-to-9-week smoothing spreads the spike over late April and May.
- **The surface therefore puts its effort peak for the main bands in late April.** EVIDENCE.md:16
  gives morels March to May. A forecast that holds effort constant (T7) or reads effort beside the
  weather triggers (T8) would discount reports in exactly the morel weeks.
- **North of 50 N the benchmark cannot separate effort from fruiting.** There the lichen and fungi
  shapes correlate 0.80 to 1.00, and both collapse in winter (lichen max/min 9 to 830). Lichens are
  not "visible all year" under snow, which was the premise that made them a benchmark (D103).
- **The lichen season adds little that held-out years can see.** Scored on all-fungi outings, the
  lichen season beats no season at all by 14,759 of 4.74 million (0.3%) on all licences. On CC it
  is 1,729 *worse* than no season. This is expected, because the scored counts contain fruiting.
  It is not a test of whether lichens track effort; nothing in these data can test that.

Options, for the owner and planner, not chosen here:

- **(a) Keep lichens as built.** Accept the April spike, and state it in T8.
- **(b) Keep lichens, and handle the event explicitly.** Either an event term for those dates, or
  the season estimated without them. This is a new modelling choice, so it needs a ruling and a
  re-fit.
- **(c) No seasonal effort shape.** Use cell, year and day-type only. On held-out data it scores
  almost the same as the lichen season. This changes TASKS.md T6's "Does".
- **(d) Pines, as the cited study used.** This needs a new download and its own dispatch.
